Example: one instructor's standards

These are one person’s choices, not defaults. They are here so you can see what a finished set of standards looks like. Yours will differ, and that is the point.

They come from the kit author’s own courses (several business courses at MSU), counted from the course files.

What is the same across her courses

  • Weekly rhythm. Quizzes open Monday at 12:01 AM and close, and are due, Sunday at 11:59 PM Eastern. All 40 assignment folders are due Sunday at 11:59 PM.
  • Announcements post Monday at 8:00 AM.
  • Attempts. One attempt on every quiz and project.
  • Answer order is shuffled on every multiple-choice question.
  • Extra results view. Dated quizzes and exams get a second view that opens at 12:01 AM the day after the quiz closes and shows the questions, the correct answers and the student’s own responses. Practice tests do not.
  • Annotation tools are on.
  • LockDown Browser with Monitor on every quiz, and every quiz has a time limit. She sets these by hand in D2L after each import. LockDown and Monitor do not travel in a package, and the kit can check LockDown but not Monitor, so the after-import checklist reminds you to do them.
  • Quiz names say what they cover (“Quiz – Lessons 4 and 5”), so the name moves when the schedule moves.
  • Layout. A Start Here module, then one module per dated week named “Week 1 – 8/31”. Inside a week: Overview, Read, Lecture, Practice, then the quiz and assignments.

What differs between her own courses

Even one person does not use one rule everywhere. This is why the kit asks you instead of assuming.

  • Quiz length and time. Weekly quizzes range from 15 to 25 points, 15 to 25 questions delivered from a bank, and from 20 to 30 minutes. Exams run 110 to 120 minutes.
  • Exams are windows of their own: one course opens an exam all day Wednesday, another opens a final Monday to Friday of finals week, another sets one fixed slot.
  • When the quiz falls. In a flipped course the quiz is due the Sunday after the class that covers the material. In the others it is due the same week.
  • Gradebook. Most use points with a 4.0 scale. One stays weighted on purpose.
  • Dropping the lowest quiz scores. Two lowest in some courses, one in another.

How the agent uses an example like this

Only the first part of the file (attempts, annotation tools, extra view, LockDown, calendar entry and an enforced time limit) is compared against your quizzes. The rest is kept as your notes. See what the kit checks. The example file itself is in the kit as examples/example-class-standards.json.
